# Hurry Up and Wait

An original 21-second procedural Blender short for RenderJuice's Speed contest. Nothing has been submitted.

## Three concepts compared

The published rubric weights Idea 30, Animation 25, Visuals 25 and Use of Speed 20. The comparisons below are qualitative creative judgments, not measured scores.

| Concept | Strength | Main risk | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| The Shortcut — the track folds so a slow sphere beats a fast one | An elegant spatial reveal could support strong visual design. | It could read as an unexplained trick; the speed relationship is less clear. | Reserve. |
| Deadline Factory — a clock manufactures its own seconds faster and faster | Procedural machinery makes the animation technique visible. | The payoff is abstract and may need explanation. | Reserve. |
| Hurry Up and Wait — an impatient racer and a steady courier catch the same little ferry | Readable characters, anticipation and a shared departure make speed the cause of the joke. | The premise is familiar; craft and timing must carry it. | Selected. |

## Storyboard

- 0–3 seconds: a tiny transport diorama. Red is visibly eager; cyan waits for the start. Establish a shared ferry beyond the end of their lanes.
- 3–5 seconds: red accelerates to the closed barrier. Cyan rolls steadily.
- 5–11 seconds: red's wheels whir and body fidgets while it makes no progress. Cyan approaches without hurry.
- 11–14 seconds: cyan arrives beside red. The signal changes and the gate lifts. Both board.
- 14–19 seconds: the ferry carries both at the same speed across the water. Red's frantic movement settles.
- 19–21 seconds: hold the miniature and the payoff, “SAME DEPARTURE.”

The film is a joke about waiting for a shared departure, not a quantitative claim about transportation efficiency. Its principal creative risk is the familiar “slow and steady” pattern. The miniature ferry, expressive machinery and precise pause are intended to make it memorable; actual audience validation has not been performed.

## Craft and integrity

All geometry, materials, camera motion and animation are generated for this project with Blender's bpy API. Codex wrote and revised the script. Motion is deliberately choreographed; there is no claim of rigid-body or fluid simulation. The water is a stylized static surface. No stock models, textures, external music, paid rendering or image-generation service is used. Blender's bundled Bfont supplies typography.

Low-resolution preview and representative frames will be inspected before final export. The result must have a real .blend, reproducible source, a complete video and honest WIP evidence. The script and source package are substantially part of production; they are not incidental render automation.
